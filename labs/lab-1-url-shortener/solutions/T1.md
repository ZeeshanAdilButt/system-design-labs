# T1 solution: how soon do random codes repeat?

Try the task first: [YOUR-TURN.md](../YOUR-TURN.md#t1-how-soon-do-random-codes-repeat).

## Answer

For 5 characters, the first repeat arrives after about **38,000 links** (the median of many
runs). For 6 characters it is about **300,000**. Not hundreds of millions, and not billions.

## Why it works

Every new code can match *any* code made before it, not just one. After n links there are about
n x n / 2 pairs, so the chance of a repeat grows with n squared. The formula the part uses,
`sqrt(pi x codes / 2)`, says the first repeat comes near the square root of the number of
possible codes: the square root of 916 million is about 30,000, of 56.8 billion about 240,000.
This is the birthday problem: 23 people are enough for a better than even chance that two share a
birthday, out of 365 days. The second run differs because each run is random and the table shows
a median of a few runs; it stays near the formula, never exactly on it.

## What the check should show

`the_codes.py --quick` printed this on the machine used to check these solutions (yours will be a
little different):

| code length | codes possible | links until first repeat (median) | the formula says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 14,776,336 | 5,279 | 4,818 |
| 5 | 916,132,832 | 38,940 | 37,935 |
| 6 | 56,800,235,584 | 308,632 | 298,700 |

The checker also measures the median of 15 runs for 5 characters and requires it to be within a
factor of two of the formula and between 10,000 and 100,000. It got 38,768 against a formula of
37,935.
